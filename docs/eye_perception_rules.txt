WHAT THE EYE MUST LOOK AT — perception rules for Bongard problems
==================================================================

Why this file exists
--------------------
Every agent in this system reasons only over what the eye (one vision-model call) and the ruler (OpenCV)
report. If the eye is asked the wrong questions, no amount of downstream reasoning, debate or judging can
recover: the deciding property simply never reaches the agents. On the 21 development problems, 6 were lost
before any reasoning started, because no agent ever saw the property the rule depends on.

So the eye's questions are not a detail. They come from what the literature says a solver must perceive, and
they are checked against the concepts the 100 problems actually need (section 4).


1. Sources
----------
[S1] Mikhail M. Bongard, "Problemy uznavaniya" (Moscow, 1967); English translation "Pattern Recognition"
     (Spartan Books, 1970). The original 100 problems appear in its appendix.
     https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongard_problem
[S2] Douglas R. Hofstadter, "Gödel, Escher, Bach" (1979), chapter XIX, the Bongard-problem section.
     A summary of the relevant passages: https://metarationality.com/bongard-meta-rationality
[S3] Harry E. Foundalis, "Phaeaco: A Cognitive Architecture Inspired by Bongard's Problems",
     PhD dissertation, Indiana University (2006), advisor D. R. Hofstadter.
     https://www.foundalis.com/res/diss_research.html
[S4] Foundalis, index of Bongard problems by the visual concepts they use (primitive and composite):
     https://www.foundalis.com/res/bps/bpidx.htm
[S5] Foundalis, Phaeaco progress report on solved problems: https://www.foundalis.com/res/solvprog.htm
[S6] The answer key used here comes from the same Foundalis index, which links each problem to its solution
     (see data/bongard-classic/SOLUTIONS_PROVENANCE.md).

Honest caveat: Bongard did not publish a list of solving "tricks". His problems pair opposite concepts on
the two sides. The solving principles below are Hofstadter's and Foundalis's accounts of how people solve
them.


2. Principles, from the sources
-------------------------------
P1  Levels of description.  [S2]
    Solvers start from simple figures and build up "properties and relationships", then "properties and
    relationships of properties and relationships". "Choosing a vocabulary, at the right level of
    description, for describing relevant factors, is key."
    -> The eye describes every panel at four levels: PARTS, OBJECTS, RELATIONS, WHOLE.

P2  Seeing the whole in the parts.  [S3]
    A solver must "see known and familiar shapes as if they were something else", e.g. "a collection of
    dots arranged in a circle as if they were a circle".
    -> A WHOLE level: what the parts form together, how they are grouped, where they lie.

P3  Sameness detection.  [S2]
    Things can be "the same on some level while different on another". Hofstadter's solver uses
    sameness-detectors.
    -> A SAMENESS level: are the objects in a panel the same shape, size, fill, or identical copies?

P4  Filtering and focusing.  [S2]
    "Nearly everything you could say about a diagram is irrelevant." Generating more description does not
    solve a problem; the solver must decide where to focus.
    -> The eye stays exhaustive (it cannot know what matters). Focusing is done neutrally, by code, AFTER
       perception: a property that is the same in all 12 panels cannot be the rule; only what varies across
       the 12 panels is a candidate dimension. The code never says which side a panel is on.

P5  Primitive concepts.  [S4]
    Foundalis indexes the problems by primitive concepts (closedness, symmetry, number, clustering,
    collinearity, convex hull, ...). A composite concept such as "equilateral triangle" decomposes into
    primitives (vertex, angle, length, sameness of feature, number).
    -> The eye uses a FIXED checklist that covers the primitives the 100 problems need, so it reports
       systematically instead of describing only what stands out.

P6  Descriptions are tentative.  [S2]
    "The way a picture is represented is always tentative"; "a high-level description can be restructured".
    -> LOOK AGAIN: after the first round, agents may send the eye back with targeted questions (one extra
       vision call, same shuffled grid). The eye answers per panel; the agents do the reasoning.

P7  The two sides are opposite concepts.  [S1][S4]
    Bongard's right side usually shows the opposite concept, not merely "anything else".
    -> Answers name what IS on each side ("<X>" vs "<Y>"), not "<X>" vs "no <X>".


3. Rules for the eye
--------------------
E1  Describe, never solve. The eye sees the 12 panels shuffled into one grid labelled P1..P12 and does not
    know which panels are left or right. It never looks for a rule or a grouping between panels.
E2  Every panel gets the same checklist (section 5), at all four levels plus sameness.
E3  Exhaustive, not salient: report EVERY pair of objects, not the most noticeable relation. (On the
    development set the earlier eye gave no above/below at all on 59% of two-figure panels where one figure
    was clearly higher, although it was right every time it did report one.)
E3b Measured in practice (21 development problems): even with this rule the eye still gave no above/below on 21 of
    32 two-figure panels. So vertical and horizontal placement also comes from the ruler, which names each figure by
    its outline and says where it sits relative to the next one.
E4  Describe distractors too. 13 of the 100 problems contain irrelevant elements [S4 "Noise"]; the eye
    reports them, and the agents decide what to ignore.
E5  Exact measurements belong to the ruler (size, position, long-axis direction, elongation, convexity,
    corners, corner angles). The eye does not repeat them, so no fact has two sources.
E6  Leave out defaults to keep the reply short: the eye's output is most of the vision cost.
E7  Examples in the eye's prompt use placeholders (<shape>, <object>), never an actual answer from the set.

Rules for the look-again questions
E8  A question is about what is visible in ONE panel and is answered the same way for all 12 panels
    (a number, yes/no, or a few words). It never mentions left, right or sides.
E9  All distinct questions from all agents go into the single look-again call (duplicates merged).
E10 The answers are added to every agent's facts, labelled as a second look.

Still not covered (16 problems: 16, 43, 52, 54, 55, 61, 67, 68, 69, 70, 72, 74, 75, 78, 81, 100):
  tracing along a path, clockwise/counterclockwise turns, imaginary points, the two regions a curve makes,
  semiplanes of a line, tree trunks, separable/entangled, curve endings, topological specificity. These need
  following a path or reasoning about imagined constructions; the look-again step is the only route to them.

Concepts reported before and still reported: shape, fill, number, inside, overlapping, closed/open,
symmetry, area texture, existence, arrows, pointing (eye); size, position, horizontal/vertical, slope,
elongation, convexity, indentation/protrusion, angles, cusps, corners (ruler).


5. The checklist (what the eye reports for every panel)
-------------------------------------------------------
PARTS      number of separate lines/curves; any open line; a line crossing itself; number of crossing
           points; number of free line ends; number of branching points; line texture (dashed, dotted,
           wavy, zigzag) or area hatching direction
OBJECTS    type, number of straight sides, solid fill, pointing direction, has a hole, long/short (lines),
           triangle kind (equilateral, isosceles, right)
COUNT      number of separate objects; count per kind when there are several kinds
RELATIONS  for EVERY pair: inside, outside, touching, overlapping, joined, above/below, left/right,
           between, near/far, parallel/perpendicular, pointing to, in front of/behind, larger/smaller
WHOLE      number of groups (clusters); items lie on one straight line or curve; what the parts form
           together; shape of the outline around everything; where small items lie relative to a closed
           outline (inside / outside / on it); regular or irregular step-by-step change; nested copies
SAMENESS   objects in the panel have the same shape / size / fill; identical copies; similar shapes
SYMMETRY   mirror-symmetric or not
NOTE       at most 10 words, only for something important not covered above

RULER (OpenCV, free, exact): size, position, long-axis direction (vertical, horizontal, diagonal rising or
falling), elongation, convexity, number of corners, acute and right-angle corners, each figure named by its
outline (3-corner, 4-corner, round) and compared with the next figure.

CODE (free): the list of properties that vary across the 12 panels (candidate dimensions) and those that
are the same in all 12 (cannot be the rule).
